UPDATED REMOTE WORK POLICY – Effective October 1, 2025 | Issued by: People & Culture Department | Document version: 3.2 | Replaces: Remote Work Policy v2.7 (March 2024) | 1. Purpose and Scope – This policy governs remote and hybrid work arrangements for all permanent employees and contractors working more than 20 hours per week. Temporary workers and interns are subject to their individual engagement terms. This policy does not apply to field sales roles, which are governed by the Field Sales Mobility Policy. 2. Eligibility – Remote work is available to employees who: (a) have completed their probationary period (minimum 3 months), (b) hold a role that does not require continuous on-site physical presence, (c) have achieved a performance rating of "meets expectations" or higher in the most recent review cycle, and (d) have a home workspace that meets the minimum ergonomic and connectivity requirements outlined in Annex A. Employees on a Performance Improvement Plan (PIP) are not eligible for remote work arrangements until the PIP has been successfully completed. 3. Work Location Requirements – 3.1 Hybrid employees must be present in the office a minimum of three days per week. The mandatory in-office days are Tuesday and Thursday. The third day is flexible and must be agreed with the direct manager on a monthly basis. 3.2 Fully remote arrangements (0 days in office) require approval from the department head and VP People & Culture, and are only available for roles explicitly designated as remote-eligible in the job architecture. Currently 34 roles qualify. 3.3 Employees must ensure they are reachable during core hours (09:00–15:00 local time) and must join video calls with camera on unless a medical accommodation has been granted. 4. Equipment and Connectivity – The company will provide a standard laptop, headset, and monitor for employees approved for hybrid or remote work. Additional peripherals (keyboard, docking station) are available via the IT request portal, subject to a €150 annual equipment allowance per employee. Employees are responsible for maintaining a minimum internet connection speed of 25 Mbps download and 10 Mbps upload. The company will reimburse up to €30 per month toward home internet costs upon submission of receipts via the expense system. 5. Health, Safety, and Ergonomics – All remote employees must complete the online DSE (Display Screen Equipment) self-assessment within 30 days of this policy taking effect and annually thereafter. Employees who identify ergonomic risks must report them to their manager and People & Culture. The company may arrange a home workspace assessment at company cost if significant ergonomic concerns are identified. 6. Data Security and Confidentiality – Remote employees must not work on confidential company data in public spaces without using an approved VPN. All devices must be encrypted and have up-to-date endpoint protection software installed. Printing confidential documents at home is prohibited unless a secure shredding arrangement is in place. Violations of data security requirements may result in immediate revocation of remote work privileges. 7. Working Hours and Availability – Remote employees are expected to maintain the same contracted working hours as their in-office counterparts. Time zone differences of more than 3 hours from the employee's primary team location require explicit manager approval. Overtime for remote workers follows the same approval process as for on-site employees. 8. Performance and Accountability – Managers are responsible for setting clear outcome-based objectives for remote team members. Performance will be assessed on outputs, not presence. Managers must hold a minimum of one formal check-in per week with remote direct reports. 9. Policy Review – This policy will be reviewed annually or following any significant change in labor law or company structure. Employees may submit feedback to peopleandculture@company.internal. Incident Summary – On August 14 at 14:37 CET, the Security Operations Center (SOC) detected anomalous login activity on a corporate email account belonging to a senior sales employee (account anonymized as User-A). Within 90 minutes, the account was used to send 23 internal emails containing a link to a credential harvesting site. Three employees clicked the link and entered their credentials before the phishing site was taken down at 16:02 CET. 2. Timeline of Events – 14:37: SOC alert triggered by impossible travel detection (login from Germany and Singapore within 11 minutes). 14:42: SOC analyst confirmed account compromise; initiated account isolation. 14:55: IT Security contacted User-A directly; confirmed device was in Germany and account was not being used by User-A. 15:10: Compromised account suspended. Email recall attempted for 23 phishing emails; 17 of 23 successfully recalled. 15:20: Three employees reported clicking the link. Credentials of affected employees immediately reset. MFA enforced on all three accounts. 16:02: Phishing domain taken down via abuse report to registrar. 16:30: All-hands security alert sent to company. 17:15: Forensic investigation launched. 3. Root Cause Analysis – Initial compromise of User-A's account was traced to a password reuse incident. User-A had used the same password on a third-party service that suffered a data breach in July 2025 (breach was publicly disclosed August 2). User-A had not yet changed the corporate password. The corporate email account did not have MFA enforced due to an oversight in the MFA rollout project (User-A was in a group excluded during phased rollout and not subsequently added). 4. Impact Assessment – Three employee accounts temporarily compromised. No evidence of data exfiltration from any of the three accounts based on DLP log review. No customer data was accessed. Email system performance was not affected. Estimated staff time lost: approximately 45 person-hours across IT Security, SOC, and affected employees. Reputational risk: low (incident was contained internally). 5. Remediation Actions Completed – Immediate: Compromised accounts secured and MFA enforced. Phishing domain taken down. All-company security alert issued. Short-term: MFA enrollment audit completed August 18 – identified 47 additional accounts without MFA; all enrolled by August 21. Password reset required for all employees whose accounts were created before May 2025 (to address password reuse risk). Long-term: Full MFA deployment review scheduled; policy updated to make MFA mandatory with no exclusions effective September 1. 6. Lessons Learned – MFA deployment tracking was inadequate. Phased rollout lacked a verification step to confirm 100% coverage. Third-party breach monitoring alerts were not linked to password reset reminders. 7. Recommendations – Implement automated tooling to detect third-party breach exposure and trigger mandatory password resets. Review all phased security rollouts to ensure no accounts are excluded without documented exception. Conduct phishing simulation exercise in September 2025. TECHNICAL SPECIFICATION – Real-Time Notification Service v2.0 | Document Status: Draft for Review | Author: Felipe Rojas (Senior Engineer) | Reviewers: Tomoko Hayashi (Architecture), Aisha Kofi (Security), Marcus Webb (Engineering Lead) | Last Updated: September 3, 2025 | 1. Overview – This document specifies the design of the Real-Time Notification Service (RTNS) v2.0, which replaces the current polling-based notification system. The current system polls the database every 10 seconds per connected client, causing O(n) database load at scale. RTNS v2.0 will use a WebSocket-based push architecture with a central message broker to deliver notifications in under 500ms P95 latency. 2. Functional Requirements – FR-01: Users must receive notifications within 500ms of the triggering event under normal load (up to 5,000 concurrent connections). FR-02: Notifications must be delivered at least once (at-least-once delivery semantics). FR-03: Users who reconnect after a disconnect must receive all missed notifications from the past 24 hours. FR-04: The system must support notification types: task assignment, mention, status change, comment, and system alert. FR-05: Notification preferences (per-user, per-type enable/disable) must be honored without additional round trips. FR-06: The service must expose a REST API for other services to publish notification events. 3. Non-Functional Requirements – NFR-01: 99.9% uptime SLA. NFR-02: Horizontal scaling without service restart. NFR-03: No notification loss during rolling deployments. NFR-04: Encryption in transit (TLS 1.2+). NFR-05: Audit logging of all published events. 4. Architecture Design – 4.1 Components: (a) Event Publisher API – REST endpoint receiving events from upstream services. Validates schema, enriches with user preference lookup, publishes to message broker. (b) Message Broker – Apache Kafka with 3-partition topic per notification type. Retention: 48 hours. Consumer group: RTNS-delivery. (c) Delivery Workers – Stateless Node.js workers consuming from Kafka. Each worker maintains a pool of WebSocket connections. Target: 1,000 connections per worker. (d) WebSocket Gateway – Load-balanced entry point for client connections. Sticky sessions via consistent hashing on user ID. (e) Preference Cache – Redis cache (TTL: 5 minutes) for user notification preferences to avoid DB hits on each event. 4.2 Data Flow: Upstream service → Event Publisher API → Kafka → Delivery Worker → WebSocket Gateway → Client. 4.3 Persistence: Notification history stored in PostgreSQL with 30-day retention. Index on (user_id, created_at DESC). 5. Open Questions – OQ-01: Should we support fallback to server-sent events (SSE) for clients that cannot maintain WebSocket connections? (Decision needed by Sep 15) OQ-02: What is the maximum acceptable notification history query window? Product team has not confirmed. OQ-03: How should we handle notification delivery when a user is logged into multiple devices simultaneously? Deliver to all sessions or most-recent only? OQ-04: Rate limiting policy for the Event Publisher API – proposed 1,000 events/second per publishing service, but this has not been validated against current event volumes. 5. Security Considerations – JWT authentication required for WebSocket handshake. Events must be scoped to the user's organization to prevent cross-tenant data leakage. Aisha to review and sign off before implementation begins. 6. Estimated Effort – Event Publisher API: 4 days. Kafka setup and schema registry: 2 days. Delivery Workers: 8 days. WebSocket Gateway integration: 3 days. Preference Cache: 2 days. Testing and hardening: 5 days. Total: 24 engineering days (2 engineers, ~2.5 weeks). Please list all open questions in this specification that must be resolved before development begins, and for each one briefly explain why it matters.

ANNUAL PERFORMANCE REVIEW GUIDELINES – Manager Reference Document | Effective: Review Cycle 2025 (October–November) | Issued by: People & Culture | Version: 4.1 | 1. Purpose – This document provides managers with detailed guidance for conducting annual performance reviews. Reviews assess employee contributions over the full calendar year and inform compensation decisions, promotion recommendations, and development planning. 2. Review Timeline – October 6: Self-assessment forms open for employees. October 20: Self-assessments due from employees. October 21: Manager review forms open. November 7: Manager reviews due. November 10–21: Calibration sessions by department. November 24: Employees notified of final ratings. December 1–15: Development conversations and goal-setting for 2026. 3. Rating Scale – 5: Exceptional – Consistently exceeded all objectives; demonstrated impact significantly beyond role scope. 4: Strong – Met or exceeded most objectives; positive impact on team and business. 3: Meets Expectations – Achieved core objectives; met the standards expected for the role. 2: Needs Development – Partially met objectives; specific development actions required. 1: Unsatisfactory – Did not meet core objectives; performance concerns require formal action. Distribution guidance: ratings of 5 should represent no more than 10% of team; ratings of 1 or 2 combined should represent no more than 15%. Managers should discuss any anticipated distribution anomalies with their HR Business Partner before calibration. 4. Writing Effective Reviews – 4.1 Use the STAR format (Situation, Task, Action, Result) for performance examples. Vague statements such as "works hard" or "is a team player" are not acceptable. Each rating must be supported by at least two specific examples. 4.2 Avoid common biases: recency bias (overweighting recent months), halo effect (one strong area inflating overall assessment), affinity bias (rating employees who are similar to you more favorably). 4.3 Distinguish between performance (what was achieved) and potential (readiness for greater responsibility). Only performance should influence the annual rating. Potential should inform the development conversation. 5. Compensation Guidance – Merit increase budgets will be communicated to managers by HR Business Partners by October 28. Merit increases are linked to performance ratings: Rating 5: 5–8% increase pool. Rating 4: 3–5%. Rating 3: 1–3%. Ratings 1–2: 0%. Increases above the range require VP approval. Promotion recommendations must be submitted separately via the Promotion Nomination form by November 7. 6. Calibration Process – Calibration sessions are mandatory for all managers with three or more direct reports. The purpose is to ensure consistency across teams. Managers must prepare a one-paragraph summary for each employee rated 4 or 5 and each employee rated 1 or 2. HR Business Partners will facilitate calibration sessions. Rating changes post-calibration require HRBP sign-off. 7. Employee Communication – Final ratings must be communicated by managers in a face-to-face or video meeting, not via email or messaging. Managers should allocate at least 45 minutes for the review conversation. Employees have the right to submit a written rebuttal within 10 business days of receiving their rating.
